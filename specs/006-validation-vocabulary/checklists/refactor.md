# Refactor Safety Checklist: Validation Vocabulary

**Purpose**: Test whether the requirements pin down tightly enough (a) what may
and may not change in the validator's output, and (b) what counts as a finished
migration. This feature succeeds when almost nothing changes, so a requirement
that fails to say precisely what "almost" covers is the defect that lets a
silent regression through.

**Created**: 2026-09-07

**Reviewed**: 2026-09-07 (agent-assisted evaluation, at the reviewer's request)

**Feature**: [spec.md](../spec.md)

**Audience & timing**: reviewer, at PR, before `/speckit-implement`.

**Status**: 32 of 32 satisfied. The first review found 12 open, 6 material and 6
low impact. All 12 were amended into the requirements on 2026-09-07, in two
passes: the 6 material ones first, then the 6 low-impact ones on a second
request. See [Findings](#findings) for what was found and
[Resolution](#resolution) for what each amendment changed.

**Note**: This custom checklist is generated by the `/speckit-checklist` command based on feature context and requirements.

**Review Ownership**: This checklist is a reviewer-owned requirements-quality review artifact. Mark an item `[x]` only when the reviewer determines the requirements-quality criterion is satisfied.

**Marker Semantics**: `[x]` means the criterion has been reviewed and satisfied for requirements quality. It does not mean implementation work is complete.

## Findings

Twelve open items at first review. Six were material, in the sense that leaving
them would let a regression through or leave a promise overstated. Six were low
impact and are recorded so the decision about them is deliberate rather than
accidental.

The findings are kept below as written, in the present tense of the review, so
the record shows what was wrong rather than only what was done about it. See
[Resolution](#resolution) for the current state of each.

### Material (all six closed by amendment)

**CHK003—SC-003 states an outcome nothing obliges anyone to verify, and the
existing suite cannot establish it.**

This is the most consequential finding, and it is not a matter of wording.
Measured against the current tree:

- The source emits **63 distinct literal `expected` strings**.
- Tests name **9** of them in an `expected == "…"` assertion, and **19** if
  whole-`ValidationProblem` comparisons are counted too.
- So **54 of 63 are named by no test at all.**

Among the 54 are `"a non-empty string"` (the wording FR-009a makes canonical),
`"a table"`, `"a table with label and class"`, `"a [task] table"`,
`"a [pseudo-hex] table"`, and `"a mustering-out table"`. Those last five are
precisely the strings the `require_dict` conversions pass through, so the
highest-risk conversions in the migration are the least covered by the net that
is supposed to catch them.

SC-004 says the suite passing is the evidence. For 54 messages it is not
evidence of anything. `quickstart.md` supplies a before/after `--json` diff
procedure, but a quickstart is a validation guide, not an obligation, and
nothing in the requirements says the comparison must be performed.

**CHK007—the exposure analysis omits the contract corpus, which does pin
validation message text.**

The Assumptions section enumerates tests, golden output, and documented
examples. It never names `tests/contract/`. That corpus does pin
`ValidationProblem` `found` and `expected` values: `test_json_contract.py:192-193`,
`205-206`, `241-242`, `354-355`.

The conclusion survives. The pinned strings are `"a well-formed TOML document"`
and the two registry-resolution messages, none of which either wording change
touches, and the registry checks are excluded by FR-020. But the project's own
CLAUDE.md carries `changing --json output breaks tests/contract/` as a standing
gotcha, and a requirements set that reasons about message exposure without
naming that corpus reached the right answer without checking the place the
project itself flags.

**CHK008—"expresses the same check inline" has no criterion, and there is a
live case the existing rules do not settle.**

FR-015 obliges conversion of inline sites; `data-model.md` lists eleven. Neither
gives a rule a reviewer could apply to a twelfth. That is not hypothetical:

`names._require_name_array` (`names.py:70-78`) hand-inlines a non-empty-string
check per array element, emitting `found` and `expected="a non-empty string"`,
the same shape `require_string` owns. Is it "a string check expressed inline",
which FR-015 says convert? Or part of "a shared check for arrays of parsed
elements", which FR-020 says do not build? The two requirements point opposite
ways at the same lines, and nothing adjudicates.

`data-model.md` resolves it by listing the helper under the array exclusion, but
that is a decision recorded, not a criterion stated: the next such site gets no
guidance.

**CHK015—FR-011 and FR-005 conflict on their face.**

FR-011: "An absent key MUST be reported as 'missing'". Unconditional, binding
every check.

FR-005: the optional-boolean check, when the key is absent, "takes a declared
default … and reports no problem for it."

Read together, FR-005 violates FR-011. The intended reading is obviously that
FR-011 scopes to the *required* checks, and `contracts/schema-vocabulary.md`
gets it right per check. But FR-011 does not say so, and it is exactly the kind
of unconditional rule an implementer applies uniformly.

**CHK019, CHK020, CHK021—the guard promises more than it delivers, and its
one real safeguard is unstated.**

Three related defects in FR-014a, all in the spec's text:

- FR-014a says the guard fails when a check "is defined outside the vocabulary"
  without saying whether "defined" means a named function definition or any
  equivalent check however written. The plan resolves it as named-only.
- The limit that follows—the guard cannot catch a fresh hand-inlined
  equivalent—appears in `research.md` R8 and the plan's risk table, and
  nowhere in `spec.md`. A later reader of the requirements alone would take the
  stronger promise.
- Nothing requires the guard to be demonstrably able to fail. Every one of the
  ten existing modules in `tests/guards/` carries such a self-test
  (`test_the_guard_can_fail`, `test_the_guard_has_something_to_check`), so the
  convention exists; the requirement does not encode it. SC-004 counts the
  guard among the permitted test additions without asking for it.

The three compound: a guard whose scope is undefined, whose limit is
undisclosed, and whose ability to fail is unverified.

**CHK030—FR-018's coverage criterion does not fit two of the seven checks it
governs.**

FR-018 requires tests "covering each check's absent-key, wrong-type, boundary,
and accepted paths." Applied literally:

- `optional_bool` has no absent-key *failure* path (absence is a success that
  yields the default) and no boundary path at all.
- `unrecognized_key_problems` has none of the four. Its paths are the empty
  case, one unrecognized key, several in sorted order, and the admitted-key
  list.

`contracts/schema-vocabulary.md` specifies both correctly. FR-018's wording
would either be satisfied vacuously for these two or drive tests for paths that
do not exist.

### Low impact (closed in a second pass)

**CHK006**—FR-013 says a further divergence is "a finding to be raised" but
names no recipient, no blocking condition, and no resolution path. On a
single-maintainer project "raised" resolves to "surfaced to the maintainer", so
the practical gap is small.

**CHK022**—SC-002 says each check is defined once "across the whole source
tree" without saying whether that means `src/` or the repository. `research.md`
R8 settles it as `src/cetools/*.py`. Matters only if a test fixture ever
legitimately defines a function by one of these names.

**CHK027**—nothing defines what is expected if a structural commit is later
found to have changed a message: fix in place, revert, or new finding. Cheap to
absorb while the branch is unpushed; worth a line if this pattern recurs.

**CHK032**—one rule is called three things: "required non-empty-string check"
(FR-003), "required text field" (FR-009a), "a non-empty name" (FR-010a), plus
"required name" in User Story 1. No requirement is wrong; a reader may wonder
whether they are different rules.

## What was verified as sound

Recorded so the review is not read as uniformly negative. Nine items passed on
evidence rather than on absence of objection:

- The behavioral blast radius is enumerated exhaustively, not characterized
  (CHK001), and the string checks' three points of agreement are documented
  alongside their one difference (CHK004).
- The sites deliberately not converted are named with reasons, so an exclusion
  is distinguishable from an oversight (CHK009, CHK010).
- "Finished" is defined without reference to line numbers, which begin drifting
  at the first edit (CHK011).
- The required-table check's soundness is tied explicitly to the assumption it
  rests on, rather than asserted (CHK017).
- FR-014a carries its own rationale, which is what should stop a later
  maintainer deleting the guard as redundant (CHK023).
- FR-019 states the no-changelog-for-structural rule as a prohibition rather
  than leaving it to inference (CHK026).
- The mechanical comparison of the duplicates is tied to a specific method and
  to the earlier claim it corrected (CHK029).

## Recommended spec amendments

*Applied. Retained as the record of what was proposed; see
[Resolution](#resolution) for what each became.*

Each maps to one or more open items. Together they close all six material
findings; none requires reopening a settled decision.

1. **Add an FR requiring the before/after message comparison** (CHK003).
   Something to the effect that the unchanged-message obligation must be
   established by comparing validator output over the invalid-fixture corpus
   before and after each structural change, since the suite asserts 9 of 63
   emitted messages. This is the one amendment that changes what gets *done*,
   not just what is written.
2. **Extend the exposure assumption to name `tests/contract/`** (CHK007), with
   the finding that it pins validation text but none of the changed text.
3. **Give FR-015 a criterion** for what counts as an inline expression of a
   check, and adjudicate the `_require_name_array` element check explicitly
   (CHK008).
4. **Scope FR-011 to the required checks**, so FR-005 is not a violation of it
   (CHK015).
5. **Amend FR-014a** to define "defined" as a named definition, state the
   inline-duplicate limit, and require the guard's self-test (CHK019, CHK020,
   CHK021). Optionally name the scanned tree (CHK022).
6. **Reword FR-018's coverage criterion** so it fits all seven checks, for
   instance by requiring each check's own outcome table from the contract to be
   covered exhaustively (CHK030).
